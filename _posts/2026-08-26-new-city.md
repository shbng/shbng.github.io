Hmmmm. So here I am. Back in the east. My sister took me to Goa, more like had to but that's a different story. The lesson from that trip (atleast it materialized during the trip) is that never judge a trip, tour, travel decision on the first few moments. No matter how upsetting things seem in different ways, it's just going to be a non-equilibrium state. And that by definition is going to be fleeting, disturbing and anxious. 

I have written a lot about how I feel about this move, to go far far away from home for a **self-funded** project. 

But neverthless, here I am chasing opportunities, chasing dreams. I am really excited to go see the interiors of TCG Crest actually. And I am also quite nervous about facing people there. I am sure it will be alright. Afterall, it's an academic setting in the end! So I should feel safer than at home in that aspect. 

Of course, I am also excited about the project. I mean, to get what you want is a bit rare for me, so yeah! June-July work paid off! Ah, the sheer number of times I was noting the feeling of pushing without support. But the fun in it kept me going, along with it's promising alignment to low-dimensional topology.

So I entered the city yesterday. Mind you, this is however a different part of Kolkata. It does't have oldage written (embracing?) on its face! If anything it's all new. It's an IT neighborhood along with some college level Education hotspot. But it's not that new either.

Of course, I travel to that part of Hyderabad very often. So I imagines wided roads. Cleaner traffic. But nope. 

This part of Kolkata echoes with the hustle and bustle ofa *chasing* city. 

On a glance at a distance, it's more like the Patia of Bhubaneswar - the Patia, KIIT Chowk, Infocity boasting colleges stretching over the city square. And this glance is particularly justified considering the Salt lake city, Kolkata is also a *planned* satellite town. 

However, the roads are small. Bhubneswar has wider roads atleast in proportion to the population/traffic. The bustle of Sector V is honestly unbearable during the peak times. Yesterday I walked around the area around 4:30-5:30pm. And today I left at 6:30am to breathe in the city, and the time around 8:30am to 10:00am is once again simply disturbing. 

As a flaneur walkiong by, it's just too much. It's of course too much. It's hard to comprehend the numbness of footpath encroacher towards all of this. Every thing still rings and smells in my head. Yes, the smoking. You pass by the people smoking in the morning, and in the night.

But it doesn't matter. Once you figure out a way to escape the humidity and the traffic, you end up in your offices, colleges, research labs. Of course, the third space of work and home is just despicable. India is stuck chasing the space called work. And it then chases the space called home. Un fortunately, the in between is just despicable. Hence fueling the chase from one to another. 

So, it doesn't make any sense to exist outside during those times. The worst time to be a pedestrian, although the one time that actually demads the people to move on their legs more than any other time. 

The residential area of Salt lake city is amazing though. Checkered roads, colonies with ample green *cover*. A delightful place to run and to simply take a stroll in the freshness of a morning and the calm setting of sun fading into the night.

So, yeah there's only one way to navigate as a pedestrian during those times. Numb yourself. If you are doing just a quick bike ride, great. But even then the traffic and driving is just on another level. 

The only thing that calmed me after experience this whole disturbance was the fact that, it's all going to only last for less than 15 minutes on a bike or half an hour by walk (if the weather is good enough to do that). Other than that you chose the places to go - you stay at the office and home for most of the time, and everything is just transitory.

But I am a person who find third spaces enriching. They tap on to my primal instincts and genuinely effect my productivity too! And as usual, we build those third spaces within walls escaping the consequences of poor governance by the state.


Okay, that's too much of negative aspects of my city experience so far.
